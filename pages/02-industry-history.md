# Global Cycling Industry History
## India | Europe | USA — From Origins to the E-Bike Era
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-08

## PART 1: THE BICYCLE IS INVENTED (Europe, 1817–1890s)

The bicycle was born in Germany. Baron Karl von Drais invented the "Laufmaschine" (running machine / dandy horse) in 1817 — a wooden, pedal-less two-wheeler pushed by feet. He publicly demonstrated it in Mannheim in 1817 and Paris in 1818.

The modern pedaled bicycle emerged in France in the early 1860s when Pierre Michaux and Pierre Lallement added a crank-and-pedal drive to an enlarged front wheel — the "velocipede" (later called "boneshaker" in England for its rough ride).

By the 1880s, the **Safety Bicycle** was born in England — Rover's 1885 model introduced the chain-drive, equal-sized wheels, and pneumatic tires (Dunlop, 1888). This was the template for every bicycle built since.

By 1890, the bicycle was a global mass-market product. The first global cycling boom (1890–1900) saw factories open across Britain, France, Germany, the USA, and eventually Asia.

## PART 2: EUROPE — HOW CYCLING BECAME CULTURE

### Netherlands: The World's Most Cycling Nation
- First bicycles on Dutch roads were imported from England, then Germany. In 1913, 100,000 bikes were imported from Germany — more than all Dutch production.
- WWI catalyzed domestic production. Bicycle numbers grew from 113,000 in 1900 to 861,000 by 1919.
- By **1925**, the Netherlands was a country of cyclists and cycle producers — all classes cycled, almost all cycles were produced domestically.
- Dutch producers formed a cartel from the 1920s onward, controlling prices. The government did not break it up.
- **Why the Netherlands stayed cycling:** Post-WWII, car lobbies pushed motorization everywhere. The Netherlands had a near-miss — until the **1973 Oil Crisis** forced a rethink. The Dutch government invested heavily in cycling infrastructure from the mid-1970s onward, cementing the bicycle's place in national identity.
- Today: ~23 million bikes for 17 million people. Highest per-capita bicycle ownership in the world.

### Germany: Engineering Dominance
- Germany went from bicycle importer to industrial cycling powerhouse by the early 1900s.
- Post-WWII economic recovery was partly fueled by bicycle manufacturing (a mobility necessity).
- German brands like **Kalkhoff, Cube, Riese & Müller, Stevens** built reputations for quality engineering.
- Bosch entered e-bike systems in **2011** — the single most important corporate decision in the history of the e-bike industry. Bosch legitimized e-bikes as serious engineering products, not toys. Today Bosch powers ~40% of all premium European e-bikes.
- Germany remains Europe's largest e-bike market: 2.05 million units sold in 2024.

## PART 3: INDIA — FROM IMPORTED BIKES TO LUDHIANA'S BICYCLE CAPITAL

### Pre-Independence (Before 1947)
- All bicycles imported, mostly from the UK. Indian cycling was for the elite and colonial administrators.
- **1939:** Hind Cycles Ltd. (Birla group) and Hindustan Bicycle Corporation set up India's first manufacturing facilities in Bombay and Patna — producing components, not complete bikes.

### Post-Independence Boom (1947–1975)
- The newly independent government prioritized domestic manufacturing under import substitution.
- **1949:** Sen Raleigh (UK partnership) sets up at Asansol, West Bengal. TI Cycles of India sets up at Madras (now Chennai) in partnership with Tube Investments UK — this becomes the dominant South India brand.
- **1950:** Atlas Cycle Industries, Sonepat, Haryana.
- **1951:** **Hero Cycles**, Ludhiana, Punjab — founded by the four Munjal brothers (Dayanand, Satyanand, Brijmohan Lall, Om Prakash). Started making bicycle components in a tiny shed.
- **1952:** Avon Cycles, Ludhiana.
- **Ludhiana, Punjab emerges as India's bicycle capital** — cluster of manufacturers, component suppliers, steel tube producers all within a few km radius. Still the center of Indian bicycle manufacturing today.

### Hero Cycles: India's Bicycle Story in One Company
- 1956: Hero Cycles formally incorporated.
- 1975: Hero becomes the world's largest bicycle manufacturer — a title held for decades.
- 1986: Hero Honda (motorcycles) forms — separate company, same family. The Munjals expand into two-wheelers.
- 1990s: Hero Cycles fights liberalization and cheap imports. Survives through scale and distribution.
- 2010s: Hero Cycles acquires UK brands (Raleigh, Maxima, BSA) — goes global.
- **2016:** Hero Cycles launches **Hero Lectro** — their e-bicycle brand. Today Hero Lectro holds an estimated 30–35% of India's organized e-cycle market.
- 2025: Hero Lectro sells 12,000–15,000 units/month.

### India Bicycle Market Scale (2025 context)
- India produces ~17–18 million bicycles per year — second only to China.
- Ludhiana accounts for ~70% of production.
- Bicycle penetration: approximately 1 bicycle per 3 people in rural India.
- The bicycle is still primarily a utility vehicle for the working class, not a leisure/sport product.
- **The e-bike shift is happening in urban India** — commuter replacement, last-mile logistics, premium fitness.

## PART 4: USA — FROM CYCLING BOOM TO MOUNTAIN BIKES TO E-BIKES

### The First American Cycling Boom (1890s)
- The bicycle was the first mass-market personal transportation device in the USA — before the automobile.
- The 1890s cycling boom was enormous: 4 million bikes sold in 1896 alone. Women's cycling became a symbol of emancipation.
- By 1900, the automobile began to displace the bicycle. The cycling boom collapsed.

### Mid-Century Utility and Kids' Market (1940s–1960s)
- The bicycle became primarily a children's toy in America during the post-war car era.
- Adult cycling was a fringe activity — associated with poverty or eccentricity.
- **Schwinn** dominated: the quintessential American bike brand, known for heavy, robust cruisers.

### The Mountain Bike Revolution (1970s–1980s)
- **1970s:** A group of California counterculture riders in Marin County (Joe Breeze, Gary Fisher, Tom Ritchey, Charlie Kelly) began riding modified balloon-tire bikes down Mount Tamalpais. This is the birth of mountain biking.
- **1981: Specialized launches the Stumpjumper** — the world's first mass-produced mountain bike. First batch of 250 units sells out in two weeks. This single product transforms the US and global bicycle industry.
- **1976: Trek** is founded by Dick Burke and Bevil Hogg in Waterloo, Wisconsin. Started making steel touring bikes in a barn. By 1983, Trek builds its first mountain bike (Model 850).
- **Giant Bicycles** (Taiwan, 1972) becomes Trek's contract manufacturer in the 1980s before launching its own brand. Giant's rise is the story of how Taiwan became the world's premium bicycle manufacturing hub.
- By the late 1980s, mountain bikes were 70% of US bicycle sales.

### The Road Cycling Renaissance (1990s–2000s)
- Greg LeMond wins Tour de France (1986, 1989, 1990) — American road cycling goes mainstream.
- Lance Armstrong wins 7 Tours (1999–2005) — triggers a massive road cycling boom in the USA.
- Carbon fiber frames, clipless pedals, integrated shifters — US brands drive innovation.
- Specialized, Trek, Cannondale become global premium brands, manufacturing primarily in Taiwan/China.

### The E-Bike Era (2015–Present)
- **2015:** California passes AB-1096 — first US state to adopt the Class 1/2/3 e-bike framework. 36 states follow.
- **Class definitions:**
  - Class 1: Pedal-assist only, cuts off at 20 mph (32 km/h)
  - Class 2: Throttle-capable, cuts off at 20 mph
  - Class 3: Pedal-assist only, cuts off at 28 mph (45 km/h)
- **2018–2020:** Direct-to-consumer e-bike brands explode: Rad Power, Aventon, Lectric, Ride1Up.
- **2020–2021:** COVID drives cycling demand surge. Rad Power raises $150M at $1.65B valuation.
- **2021:** ~790,000 e-bikes imported to USA (Customs data). Market accelerates.
- **2023:** Rad Power reaches 600,000 cumulative bikes sold. Peak market.
- **2024:** ~1.7 million e-bikes imported. 30% of all US bicycle market value.
- **2025 (Dec):** Rad Power Bikes files Chapter 11 bankruptcy — a warning signal about D2C unit economics at scale.
- **2026 (Mar):** Rad Power sold for $13.2M (vs. $1.65B peak valuation) — acquired by new owner pledging US manufacturing.
- **2024–2026:** IRA (Inflation Reduction Act) e-bike tax credit proposed but not passed. Consumer market remains incentive-free at federal level.
- **2025:** ~1.5M e-bikes sold in USA. E-bikes = 30% of US bicycle market by value.

### Key US Market Structure Insight
The US e-bike market has two distinct worlds:
1. **Mass-market / DTC** (Rad, Lectric, Aventon, Heybike) — under $2,000, throttle-capable, hub motor, leisure/commuter
2. **Premium IBD** (Trek, Specialized, Cannondale) — $4,000–$12,000, mid-drive, performance-oriented

The DTC segment had its shakeout with Rad's bankruptcy. The premium IBD segment remains healthy. **This bifurcation is the defining structural feature of the US market.**

## KEY CROSS-GEOGRAPHY INSIGHTS FOR e-motorad NPD

1. **Every market had the same trajectory:** utility bike → leisure/sport → premium → e-bike transition. India is 10–15 years behind Europe/USA in this arc — meaning Europe/USA patterns predict India's future.

2. **The bicycle always reflects infrastructure policy.** Netherlands chose cycling; USA chose cars. India is now choosing — PM E-Drive, EV zones, and metro expansion are the policy inflection points.

3. **Brand loyalty in bicycles is extremely high.** Hero in India, Trek/Specialized in USA, local brands in Europe. Breaking in requires either price disruption or segment creation (e.g., Specialized created the mountain bike market; Rad Power created the US mass-market e-bike segment).

4. **Taiwan and China are the world's bicycle manufacturing backbone.** Giant, Merida (Taiwan) are the OEM factories for almost every "western" brand. China is the component supplier. India has the manufacturing base (Ludhiana) but lacks the component ecosystem depth.

5. **The e-bike is not a bicycle plus a motor.** It is a new product category that happens to look like a bicycle. The companies that understood this (Bosch, Shimano, Specialized SL team) won. Those that treated it as an add-on (early low-quality Chinese e-bikes) created a credibility problem the whole industry is recovering from.
