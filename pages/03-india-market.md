## REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

### What Counts as an E-Cycle (No License/Registration Required)
- Motor: ≤250W continuous rated power
- Speed: Assisted speed ≤25 km/h
- Form: Bicycle form factor with pedals
- Battery: Advanced chemistry (LFP or NMC) required for subsidy eligibility
- Standard: AIS-174 (notified Nov 2024 by MoRTH)

**Key implication:** E-cycles under ≤250W / ≤25km/h are treated like regular bicycles for registration and licensing. This is a massive adoption advantage vs. e-scooters.

### BIS Certification
- Required for e-cycle components/batteries sold in India
- IS 13129 (and related standards) cover battery pack requirements
- UN 38.3 required for battery transport/shipping
- AIS-174 is the current benchmark for EV drivetrains

## GOVERNMENT SUBSIDY LANDSCAPE

### PM E-Drive Scheme (2024–March 2026)
- **Budget:** ₹10,900 crore total
- **For e-2Wheelers:** ₹2,500 per kWh of battery capacity, capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle
- **Condition:** Only advanced chemistry batteries (LFP, NMC) eligible
- **Runs until:** July 31, 2026 for e-2Ws
- **Note:** This applies mainly to e-scooters/motorcycles. E-cycles (bicycle form) may qualify if motor ≤250W.

### FAME-II Legacy (Ended March 2024)
- Ran 2019–2024. Subsidized e-2Ws at ₹15,000/kWh (later reduced). Drove massive initial e-scooter adoption.
- EMPS 2024 (Apr–Sep 2024, ₹778 crore) bridged between FAME-II and PM E-Drive.

### State-Level Subsidies (Selected)
| State | Subsidy | Notes |

**Critical insight:** India's subsidy structure is primarily aimed at e-scooters/motorcycles, not e-cycles. E-cycles benefit indirectly through infrastructure investment and awareness, not direct purchase subsidies at scale.

## DEMAND DRIVERS SPECIFIC TO INDIA

1. **Fuel cost sensitivity** — petrol at ₹100+/litre makes e-cycles TCO-compelling even at mid-premium prices
2. **Urban traffic + last-mile** — metro cities have 15–30 minute last-mile gaps that e-cycles solve
3. **B2B delivery boom** — Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit driver fleets are converting; 500,000+ delivery riders in top 10 cities
4. **Health & fitness** — post-COVID urban fitness wave; cycling clubs growing in Delhi, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad
5. **No license/registration** — removes friction vs. e-scooters
6. **College campus mobility** — e-cycles popular in university towns

## Demand Constraints
1. **Infrastructure** — poor cycling infrastructure outside dedicated cycling zones
2. **Safety perception** — road safety concerns limit mainstream adoption
3. **Charging ecosystem** — less relevant for e-cycles (home charging fine), but battery quality anxiety remains
4. **Price** — mass market is very price-sensitive; mid-premium requires a different consumer

## WHAT'S MISSING IN INDIA (White Spaces)

1. **Premium gravel/adventure e-bike** — does not exist from an Indian brand. All premium cyclists import (Specialized, Trek, Giant) at 80%+ customs duty.
2. **Integrated-battery design** — almost no Indian e-cycle has a hidden/integrated battery. All are afterthought battery placements.
3. **Women-specific e-cycle** — massive underserved segment (step-through, lighter weight, urban-specific)
4. **B2B fleet management software + hardware** — IoT, GPS, fleet analytics — missing from most e-cycle brands
5. **Cargo e-bike** — non-existent from Indian brands despite last-mile logistics boom
