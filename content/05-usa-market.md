## REGULATORY FRAMEWORK — THE CLASS SYSTEM

### History
- **2015:** California passes AB-1096 — first state to adopt a three-class e-bike framework. Became the national template.
- **2024:** 36 states + DC use the three-class system. 28 states have adopted Class 3 regulations.
- **Federal level:** No unified federal e-bike law exists. CPSC regulates e-bikes as consumer products (750W max, 20 mph max on throttle under federal definition). Individual states determine trail/road access.

### The Three Classes

| Class | Motor | Top Assisted Speed | Throttle? | Trail/Path Access |
|-------|-------|--------------------|-----------|------------------|
| **Class 1** | Pedal-assist only | 20 mph (32 km/h) | No | Most multi-use trails |
| **Class 2** | Any | 20 mph (32 km/h) | Yes (up to 20 mph) | Fewer trails |
| **Class 3** | Pedal-assist only | 28 mph (45 km/h) | No | Roads/bike lanes mainly |

**Class distribution (2025):** Class 1 = 61% of market | Pedal-assist = 75% of market

### Key Difference vs. Europe
- USA allows **750W** motors (vs. Europe's 250W limit)
- USA allows **28 mph** Class 3 (vs. Europe's 25 km/h)
- USA has **no national e-bike subsidy** (IRA e-bike tax credit proposed but never passed)
- USA has **no type approval** requirement — just CPSC compliance
- Result: US market has much larger, more powerful e-bikes than Europe

## THE RAD POWER STORY — CRITICAL CASE STUDY

Rad Power was the poster child of the US e-bike boom and bust. It teaches you everything about the risks in this market.

**Rise:**
- Founded 2007 by Mike Radenbaugh (custom e-bikes)
- 2015–2020: Bootstrapped growth, DTC model, 6x growth
- Feb 2021: Raises $150M at $1.65B valuation (peak of pandemic cycling boom)
- 2023: Reaches 600,000 cumulative e-bikes sold. Largest US e-bike brand.

**Fall:**
- Post-2021: Consumer cycling demand normalizes. Inventory buildup.
- 2022–2023: Severe price competition from cheap Chinese imports (Lectric selling at $799 vs Rad's $1,500+)
- 2024: Warehouse fire destroys inventory. Supply chain disruptions. Consumer spending shifts.
- Nov 2025: Announces "significant financial challenges"
- Dec 2025: Files Chapter 11 bankruptcy
- Mar 2026: Sold for $13.2M (vs. $1.65B valuation — 99% value destruction)

**Lessons:**
1. DTC e-bikes are a commodity market at sub-$2,000. Brand loyalty is weak.
2. Scale without profitability is a death trap. Rad prioritized units over margin.
3. Chinese competition is relentless at the mass-market price point.
4. The premium segment (Trek, Specialized) survived fine — they have dealer networks and brand equity.

## MARKET STRUCTURE — THE TWO WORLDS

### World 1: Mass Market DTC (Under $2,000)
- Hub motor, throttle-capable, Class 2 primarily
- Online-first, Facebook/Instagram advertising
- Commoditized — price competition brutal
- Post-Rad, Lectric is the new mass-market leader
- Typical buyer: 35–55 year old, urban/suburban, recreational

### World 2: Premium IBD ($4,000+)
- Mid-drive motor (Bosch, Shimano), no throttle
- Dealer-sold, test rides important
- Brand and performance matter
- Class 1 or Class 3
- Typical buyer: 40–60 year old, higher income, performance/commuter

**The $2,000–$4,000 gap is largely empty.** This is the most interesting price range for potential disruption.

## KEY TRENDS (2024–2026)

### 1. Tariffs Reshaping the Market
- US imposed significant tariffs on Chinese goods (Section 301 tariffs) — e-bikes face 25%+ tariff
- Lectric opened a Phoenix assembly plant partly to navigate this
- "Made in USA" is a growing marketing angle post-Rad bankruptcy

### 2. E-Cargo Growing Fast
- Last-mile delivery boom (Amazon, UPS, USPS all piloting e-cargo)
- Urban cargo bike market growing 20%+ annually
- Very underdeveloped compared to Europe

### 3. Federal Policy Uncertainty
- IRA e-bike tax credit ($1,500 max) proposed but not enacted
- If passed, it would immediately transform the $1,500–$3,500 segment
- Watch Congress — this is a policy risk/opportunity

### 4. Trail Access Expansion
- More US states opening multi-use trails to Class 1 e-bikes
- E-MTB legitimization ongoing — opens new market segments

### 5. Insurance / Theft
- E-bike theft is exploding in US cities
- Demand for GPS tracking, smart locks growing
- Some states beginning to require liability insurance for Class 3

## RELEVANCE TO e-motorad

The USA is the third geography priority. Here's how to think about it:
- **Entry timing:** Not immediately. Build India and Europe first.
- **Right segment for India-origin brand:** The $2,000–$4,000 gap, mid-market quality, targeting price-sensitive performance buyers
- **Not** the mass-market DTC space (Rad just proved that's a bloodbath)
- **Regulatory path:** Design for EU (250W/25km/h) first. US Class 1 compliance is then easy (just deprogram speed limit or add firmware switch).
- **Watch the federal tax credit.** If it passes, the mid-market opens dramatically.
